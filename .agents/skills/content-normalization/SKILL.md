# Content Normalization

## Purpose

Use this skill when visible content is inconsistent, mixed in tone, mixed in language, too repetitive, too placeholder-like, or structurally weak.

This skill improves the quality and consistency of public-facing copy without changing the core product direction.

Typical use cases:
- mixed PT-BR and English labels
- inconsistent CTA language
- placeholder footer text
- repetitive section descriptions
- weak headings
- skill/technology presentation feels dumped
- copy sounds too generic or too rough

Do not use this skill for:
- inventing new case studies
- product strategy changes
- fake proof generation
- broad visual redesign
- major IA restructuring without story support

---

## Required context before use

Read:

1. `AGENTS.md`
2. `PRD.md`
3. `brand.md`
4. `decisions.md`
5. `progress.txt`
6. the active story

If the language strategy has already been decided in `decisions.md`, follow it strictly.

---

## Objective

Normalize visible content so it feels:
- consistent
- credible
- curated
- mature
- readable
- aligned with the chosen language strategy
- aligned with the premium identity

Preserve:
- truthful claims
- technical seriousness
- concise tone
- the section’s actual purpose

---

## Normalization checklist

### 1. Language consistency
Check:
- headings
- navigation labels
- CTA text
- footer text
- section intros
- metadata labels

Do not leave accidental language mixing in production-facing UI unless explicitly intended.

---

### 2. Tone consistency
Check whether the copy feels:
- too casual in one section
- too corporate in another
- too academic in another
- too placeholder-like
- too buzzword-heavy
- too verbose

Normalize toward:
- direct
- concise
- mature
- calm
- technically credible

---

### 3. Remove filler
Tighten:
- vague adjectives
- repeated ideas
- generic “build amazing things” phrases
- empty claims
- bloated intros
- unnecessary label repetition

Keep the strongest statements and remove the rest.

---

### 4. Curate technical presentation
For stacks, tools, studies, or capabilities:
- group related content
- reduce repetition
- prioritize signal
- label things clearly
- avoid raw dump presentation

Content should feel selected, not spilled.

---

### 5. Preserve truthfulness
Never invent:
- clients
- testimonials
- outcomes
- performance claims
- metrics
- awards
- case-study facts

If proof is weak, improve framing instead of fabricating substance.

---

### 6. Make CTA language credible
Review CTA and closing language:
- should be direct
- should be calm
- should not sound needy
- should not sound like generic SaaS filler
- should support real professional contact

---

## Anti-patterns

Do not:
- replace everything with generic polished corporate copy
- erase the personal/technical character of the portfolio
- over-market the site
- invent prestige signals
- turn concise text into long polished fluff
- normalize by making everything sound the same and lifeless

---

## Acceptance review

This pass is successful when:
- visible language is consistent
- tone is more coherent across sections
- filler is reduced
- content feels more curated
- CTA language is stronger
- truthfulness is preserved
- the portfolio sounds more mature and intentional

---

## Validation expectations

When content changes are made:
- verify the changed copy in context
- check desktop and mobile for line breaks and density
- verify no accidental language mixing remains in touched areas
- document the normalization in `progress.txt`
