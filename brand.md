# brand.md

## Brand purpose

This portfolio should present Jordano as a **serious technical builder** with strong execution ability, product thinking, visual taste, and commercial clarity.

The brand should communicate:

- technical depth
- maturity
- clarity
- premium taste
- seriousness
- restraint
- high-agency execution
- buyer-facing usefulness

The site should not feel like a generic developer template or an overdesigned agency clone.

It should feel like a **premium personal product-facing site**.

---

## Brand essence

### Core perception

A visitor should feel:

- “this person builds real things”
- “this person is technically strong”
- “this person has taste”
- “this person can turn ideas into production”
- “this person is deliberate, not improvised”
- “this person understands how technical work supports real business needs”

### Emotional tone

The site should feel:

- confident
- calm
- deliberate
- refined
- modern
- technical
- editorial
- grounded

It should **not** feel:

- loud
- trendy for trend’s sake
- childish
- overly playful
- chaotic
- over-animated
- sterile in a corporate way
- like a mass-produced Tailwind template

## Positioning statement

Jordano is a software engineer and product-minded builder who creates serious digital experiences with technical depth, strong structure, and careful execution.

The portfolio should communicate this through composition, clarity, and proof, not through exaggerated claims.

---

## Commercial clarity

The portfolio is not only a taste signal; it is also a commercial decision surface.

Design and content should help a potential client quickly understand:

- what Jordano builds
- what kinds of problems he solves
- where his technical depth is most useful
- how to start a conversation

Avoid sections that are visually refined but commercially ambiguous.

When visual abstraction and commercial clarity conflict, clarity should win.

## Language strategy

Visible UI and section copy should follow a consistent language system.

### Rule

Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`) is the primary language for the public-facing experience.

Keep it consistent across:

- section headings
- navigation
- CTA labels
- paragraph copy
- footer text
- contact prompts
- alt text and short UI metadata when those strings are user-facing

### Allowed exceptions

- technology names, company names, and product names that are conventionally used in English
- established industry terms where translation would sound unnatural or less clear

### Avoid

- mixed-language headings without intent
- English labels with Portuguese section body, or vice versa
- placeholder language in production UI

If bilingual support is ever desired, it should be an explicit future product decision, not accidental mixing.

## Visual identity

### Overall direction

The visual system should feel like a hybrid of:

- premium portfolio
- editorial landing page
- technical product site

The design should be intentional and composed, with careful hierarchy and spacing.

The visual impression should be strong without becoming visually noisy.

---

## Visual mood

The intended mood is:

- dark and refined
- warm rather than cold
- precise rather than flashy
- premium rather than decorative
- modern rather than trendy

The visual world should feel close to:

- deep wine / dark surfaces
- cream or soft-light contrast surfaces
- elegant typography
- generous but controlled spacing
- polished composition

---

## Color direction

### Core palette intent

The palette should preserve the current identity:

- dark wine / burgundy dominant base
- cream / soft off-white contrast
- restrained supporting neutrals
- occasional subtle accents only if they reinforce hierarchy

### Color behavior rules

Use color to support:

- hierarchy
- emphasis
- surface separation
- rhythm between sections
- premium atmosphere

Do not use color as decoration without structural purpose.

### Must preserve

- dark premium foundation
- warm contrast
- identity continuity with the current hero
- strong readability

### Must avoid

- bright startup SaaS blues as dominant identity
- random gradients everywhere
- neon accents
- pastel drift that weakens seriousness
- excessive color variety section to section
- high-saturation UI noise

### Desired typographic behavior

- strong

## Layout principles

### Overall layout philosophy

The layout should feel curated, not assembled from interchangeable blocks.

Each section should have a reason to exist and a distinct role in the page narrative.

### Layout goals

- clear scan path
- strong visual rhythm
- intentional spacing
- high-quality negative space
- consistent alignment logic
- fewer stronger compositions

### Prefer

- well-structured vertical flow
- strong container discipline
- balanced asymmetry where useful
- clean grouping of related information
- sections with a clear primary focal point

### Avoid

- too many equal-weight cards
- repetitive block stacking with weak hierarchy
- sections that feel like leftovers
- awkward two-column forms
- dense walls of equal text
- layouts that look “component-generated” instead of designed

### Section purpose clarity

Each section must communicate a clearly legible purpose to a first-time visitor.

A section should not exist mainly to:

- look sophisticated
- hold abstract positioning language
- repeat signals already established elsewhere
- present loosely grouped technical information without a clear buyer-facing role

If a section cannot be explained in one sentence as part of the page narrative, it should be simplified, merged, or removed.

---

## Spacing and rhythm

Spacing should communicate quality.

### Desired spacing feel

- generous
- breathable
- intentional
- premium
- consistent

### Rules

- use spacing to create hierarchy, not just separation
- preserve enough air around major statements
- avoid cramped section interiors
- avoid sections that feel empty because spacing lacks structure
- maintain a coherent rhythm from hero to footer
- keep the hero among the airiest moments on the page; lower sections may become more concrete, but not busier or more crowded than the hero

### Section rhythm

The page should alternate intensity and rest.

That means:
- not every section should be equally dense
- not every section should use the same card pattern
- not every section should have the same visual weight

This rhythm is critical to avoiding template feel.

### Negative space discipline

Negative space should create focus, hierarchy, and compositional calm.

It must not:

- leave large inactive areas with no structural payoff
- push meaningful content into an unnecessarily narrow column
- make sections feel unfinished or underfilled
- exist only to simulate premium minimalism

High-quality spacing should support the content’s role on the page.

If a section feels empty without becoming clearer, the spacing is too loose.

---

## Surface system

Surfaces should feel restrained and premium.

### Preferred surfaces

- dark base sections
- subtle light or cream contrast sections where appropriate
- cards used selectively
- clean borders, muted dividers, or understated elevation
- light or cream surfaces used as punctuation moments, not as a reset into a different brand language

### Avoid

- too many boxed elements
- glossy or overly elevated cards
- card overload
- aggressive shadows
- visual clutter from nested containers

Cards should exist because they improve comprehension, not because every item needs a box.

### Secondary panel readability

Support panels, side notes, and secondary cards must remain clearly readable.

Avoid:

- low-contrast text on tinted light surfaces
- washed-out support blocks that feel decorative rather than useful
- subtle tonal differences that disappear on real screens

Secondary content may be quieter than the main focal point, but it must never feel faded, ghosted, or visually unresolved.

---

## Iconography and graphic elements

Icons and supporting graphics should be minimal and functional.

### Preferred

- simple icons
- restrained visual motifs
- subtle supporting lines/dividers
- visual devices that help structure information

### Avoid

- decorative icon overload
- illustration styles that clash with the premium mood
- playful graphics that undercut seriousness
- visual gimmicks used only to fill space

---

## Motion principles

Motion should support polish, not spectacle.

### Motion should be

- subtle
- smooth
- purposeful
- hierarchy-supporting
- performance-aware

### Good uses

- soft reveal on scroll
- gentle hover feedback
- slight transitions between states
- small emphasis shifts for CTA and interactive elements

### Avoid

- heavy parallax
- dramatic choreography
- constant animated movement
- large delays that hurt clarity
- flashy effects that make the site feel less mature

If motion is removed entirely, the site should still feel premium.

## Section-level brand guardrails

### Hero

The hero is currently a protected strength.

### Hero guardrails

- do not weaken the first impression
- do not dilute the premium/editorial feel
- do not introduce patterns below the hero that clash with it
- preserve the sense of confidence and quality already established

### Hero compatibility rules

- lower-page headings should support the hero, not try to out-perform it through scale, novelty, or visual loudness
- palette shifts below the hero should feel like controlled rhythm changes within the same brand family
- any light-surface moment should read as an intentional contrast break, not a new visual identity
- decorative devices introduced later on the page must stay quieter than the hero's opening impression

The rest of the site must rise toward the hero, not drag it down.

---

### Below-the-fold sections

These are the main opportunity area.

### Guardrails

- sections must feel more curated
- reduce generic portfolio patterns
- strengthen narrative continuity
- combine or simplify weak sections where beneficial
- keep premium tone consistent
- every section should feel intentional and necessary

### Composition rules

- each section should communicate one primary idea before introducing supporting detail
- prefer a small number of strong groups over broad grids of equal-weight content
- use editorial pairings such as heading plus proof, summary plus detail, or statement plus structured support
- move from broad positioning near the top toward more concrete proof lower on the page
- transitions between sections should feel deliberate, not like unrelated blocks stacked together

### Commercial interpretation rule

Below-the-fold refinement should not become abstraction for its own sake.

A section may feel editorial, but it still needs to help the visitor understand:

- what is being offered
- why it matters
- why Jordano is credible
- what to do next

Avoid elegant layouts that delay understanding without increasing perceived value.

---

### Contact / final CTA

The closing section should feel calm, premium, and real.

### Desired feel

- deliberate
- warm but professional
- easy to trust
- not desperate
- not placeholder-like

### Avoid

- loud conversion tricks
- awkward layout experiments
- overbuilt forms
- generic “let’s build something amazing” filler

### Contact section rules

The contact area must be immediately readable and action-oriented.

It should make clear:

- why someone should reach out
- what kind of conversation is useful
- which channel to use
- what kind of next step is expected

Support content inside the closing section must help conversion, not weaken it.

Avoid:

- low-contrast support cards
- decorative side panels with little practical value
- oversized empty areas around the CTA
- conceptual copy that feels unfinished

## Content presentation rules

Content should be curated, not dumped.

### Skills / tools / technologies

When presenting technical capability:

Prefer:
- grouping
- hierarchy
- narrative framing
- selected emphasis

Avoid:
- giant undifferentiated lists
- badge walls
- raw stack dumping
- repetition across multiple sections

### Projects / work

Projects should feel like selected work, not random entries.

Prefer:
- curation
- relevance
- short context
- outcome or purpose
- technical seriousness

Avoid:
- identical generic cards
- weak descriptions
- inflated claims
- fake impact language

### Technical depth presentation

Technical depth should support trust, not become an inventory exercise.

Prefer:
- clear framing
- a small number of strong technical themes
- visible relevance to real delivery
- explanation of where the depth is useful

Avoid:
- sections that read like accumulated study notes
- repeated “stack / experience / studies” signals with no clear distinction
- broad technical lists without buyer-facing meaning
- abstract expertise displays that do not support the page narrative

---

## Accessibility guardrails

Premium aesthetics cannot come at the expense of usability.

### Must maintain

- strong readable contrast
- legible body text
- visible interactive states
- logical heading structure
- reasonable responsiveness
- touch-friendly interactions where relevant

### Must avoid

- low-contrast “luxury” styling that hurts readability
- tiny body text
- visually hidden important actions
- motion that makes reading harder

### Readability priority

Subtlety is allowed, but legibility is non-negotiable.

If a panel, label, metadata block, or support card becomes difficult to read on a normal screen, the styling is too quiet and should be strengthened.

---

## Responsive brand behavior

The brand must survive responsive adaptation.

### On mobile

The site should still feel:

- premium
- intentional
- readable
- calm

### Avoid on smaller screens

- cramped complexity
- broken hierarchy
- too many side-by-side elements
- two-column layouts that become awkward
- visual clutter caused by stacking too many cards or pills

Mobile should feel like a considered composition, not a collapsed desktop layout.

## Anti-patterns

These are explicit brand violations.

Do not introduce:

- generic startup SaaS redesign patterns that erase the wine/cream identity
- shiny dashboard-style UI elements in the portfolio
- excessive badge clouds
- visual noise disguised as polish
- random gradients on every section
- overuse of glassmorphism
- over-animated section entrances
- playful dribbble-style tricks that reduce seriousness
- copied Payload aesthetics without adapting them to the current identity
- fake prestige signals
- placeholder-feeling final sections
- obvious template repetition
- low-contrast support panels that look elegant but fail in real use
- large empty side areas with no compositional payoff
- sections that feel intelligent in tone but unclear in purpose
- editorial framing that weakens selling clarity

---

## Design decision filter

Before accepting any visual or content change, check whether it satisfies these questions:

1. Does this preserve the existing identity?
2. Does this feel compatible with the hero?
3. Does this increase clarity?
4. Does this increase perceived maturity?
5. Does this avoid generic template feel?
6. Does this help present Jordano as a serious builder?
7. Is this more deliberate than what existed before?
8. Does this improve commercial understanding?
9. Is the negative space helping composition rather than creating emptiness?
10. Would a first-time visitor understand why this section exists?

If the answer to several of these is no, the change should be rejected or revised.

## Brand definition of success

The brand is being expressed correctly when the portfolio feels:

- premium from top to bottom
- visually coherent
- technically credible
- curated rather than crowded
- mature rather than performative
- distinctive without being loud
- strong enough to support professional trust
- clear enough to support commercial trust

## One-line brand directive

**Preserve the dark premium identity, protect the hero’s sophistication, and make every lower section feel curated, credible, commercially clear, and intentionally designed.**
